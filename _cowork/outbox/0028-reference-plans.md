# What real plans do

Reference study for run 0028. Written before any of the five flats was designed,
because the five are meant to come out of this rather than out of first
principles.

Ten plans were read on screen. Eight of them are labelled drawings with a printed
floor area for every room, which is why the area figures below are quoted rather
than measured. Two more were opened and are reported without a graph, because
their door positions could not be read honestly. Everything here either comes off
a drawing that was opened or is quoted from a named source. Anything that could
not be verified says so.

The best source turned out to be the datasheet series published by
Wohnbaugenossenschaften Zürich at `wbg-zh.ch`. Each datasheet carries a
Grundrissbeispiele page with fully labelled plans, per-room Bodenfläche, a scale
bar and the rent. Six of the ten plans come from four of those datasheets. Two
come from the Volkswohnung Karlsruhe plan sets, which are the German
counterpart and are drawn with door swings, so their graphs are the most
reliable of the set.

## A note on the Swiss room count

The "4.5 Zimmer" convention has no binding legal definition. Living rooms,
bedrooms, children's rooms and studies count as full rooms; kitchens and
bathrooms do not, unless the kitchen is a Wohnküche of sufficient size
(myky.ch, `https://www.myky.ch/de/recht/mietrecht-schweiz/zimmer-zaehlen-wohnung`).
The federal WBS defines a half room as a naturally lit and ventilated area of at
least 5 m² that cannot be counted as a room. Zürich asks for 12 m² for a
Wohnküche and 6 m² for a windowed dining area.

In the plans read here the pattern is that living and dining together count as
one, an open kitchen counts as the half, and each Zimmer counts as one. The
consequence is that the ".5" names the kitchen. This matters for the run because
the prompt asks for flats by room count, and room count and area do not track
each other. Else Züblin West publishes a 2.5-room flat at 68.9 m² and a
3.5-room flat at 68.6 m² on the same page of the same datasheet.

## The patterns that repeat

**The entrance always opens into a vestibule, and the vestibule is internal.** In
ten of ten the flat door opens into an Entrée or a Diele. In the Swiss plans that
vestibule sits directly against the Treppenhaus, which the drawings label. It is
a windowless room in every case. The flat door never opens into a habitable
room, and it never opens into a space that is also a room's only route to
daylight.

**The hall has two forms.** The first is an Entrée on its own, which distributes
to one or two rooms and then gives out into the living space, with everything
else hanging off the living room. Manegg, Else Züblin and the 2.5-room
Zwischenbächen work this way. The second adds a separate corridor for the
bedroom and bathroom wing, as at Obsthalden and the 4.5-room Zwischenbächen.
In both of those the corridor is entered from the living or dining room rather
than from the entrance, which was not expected.

**Circulation takes 5 to 15 per cent of the flat, median about 10.5.** Computed
from the printed areas: Else Züblin 3.5 rooms 5.4 per cent, Manegg 4.5 rooms 7.9,
Manegg 3.5 rooms 8.2, Obsthalden 10.5, Zwischenbächen 4.5 rooms 10.6,
Volkswohnung 3 rooms 11.3, Zwischenbächen 2.5 rooms 13.3, Volkswohnung 2 rooms
14.8. The two German plans sit at the top of the range because a Diele built to
DIN 18040-2 has to hold a 1.50 m turning circle.

**Every habitable room is on the facade.** This holds in all eight plans read.
The rooms allowed to be internal are the Entrée, the corridor, the bath, the
shower, the WC and the Reduit, and in two cases the kitchen. Manegg 3.5 puts the
kitchen as a galley against the internal wall of the open living space, and
Zwischenbächen 4.5 puts the kitchen and dining inboard. Obsthalden is the
clearest case of an internal core: its Bad at 4.4 m² and Dusche at 2.7 m² form a
landlocked block in the geometric centre of the plan.

**The wet rooms group into one block against one internal wall, with the kitchen
on it.** This holds in all eight. At Obsthalden the Bad and the Dusche share a
wall with each other and sit immediately below the kitchen. At Zwischenbächen 4.5
the Du/WC and the Bad/WC stack one above the other on the same wall off the
corridor, with the kitchen and dining on the other side of that same spine. At
both Manegg flats the Du/WC block sits directly against the kitchen galley. At
Volkswohnung the Bad, the WC and the washing machine niche form one rectangle
with the kitchen on the far side of its wall. No source was found that states
the plumbing shaft reason in words, so this is recorded as a consistent
geometric observation rather than as a documented rule. It is the observation
that the prompt's wet-room constraint asks for, and the plans support it without
exception.

**Room areas.** These are the printed figures across the eight plans. Combined
living and dining runs 18.2 to 32.7 m². Living alone, where the plan splits it,
runs 17.3 to 21.6. Dining alone runs 14.0 to 16.3. The largest Zimmer runs 13.3
to 15.9, and second and third Zimmer run 10.5 to 14.7. A separate or galley
kitchen runs 7.0 to 11.2. A bath or Du/WC runs 4.0 to 6.7, and a separate WC 1.75
to 2.7. An Entrée runs 3.7 to 10.1 and a separate corridor 4.8 to 6.3. A Reduit
runs 1.9 to 3.0, and a Loggia or Balkon 3.1 to 9.7.

**Room proportions in metres could not be verified.** None of the eight plans
printed a width by a depth, and the drawings were not measured against their
scale bars. Any width and depth quoted for these plans would be arithmetic on an
assumed proportion, so none is quoted here.

**Building depth could not be sourced for Switzerland.** The only quantified
source found is Pedestrian Observations, "Building Depth and Window Space"
(`https://pedestrianobservations.com/2020/11/26/building-depth-and-window-space/`),
which measures whole-building depths of urban blocks: Stockholm euroblock 13 to
14 m, Berlin Neukölln 13 m, Berlin Prenzlauer Berg 14 m with 7 m side wings,
Paris 16th 18 m. For a double-loaded perimeter block that is roughly 6 to 7 m
per side from facade to spine. The Swiss plans read here look consistent with
that order of magnitude, and they were not measured, so no facade-to-core figure
is treated as sourced.

## The four starting rules

The prompt gave four rules from earlier work and asked for each to be confirmed
or corrected. Three survive and one does not.

**Living connects to kitchen and to the hall. Holds, eight of eight.** The
kitchen is either open to the living and dining space or is literally the same
room, as at Else Züblin, whose plan labels one room "Wohnen/Essen/Küche BF
31.7 m²". The living space is reached from the Entrée in every plan. One nuance:
at Obsthalden the living side is three linked rooms, Wohnen 17.3, Essen 14.0 and
Küche 11.2, and it is the dining room that touches the Entrée while the living
room touches the kitchen. The rule holds there only if "living" is read as a
cluster rather than as one node.

**A bedroom opens off the hall and never off the living room. Contradicted.**
Three counter-examples, all Swiss and all recent. At Manegg 3.5 the 15.6 m²
Zimmer 1 has its only door onto the kitchen and living space, confirmed at door
swing level, and only Zimmer 2 opens off the Entrée. At Else Züblin 3.5 both
Zimmer, 13.7 m² each, open off the combined living, dining and kitchen room,
and the 3.7 m² Entrée serves only the Du/WC and the living space. At Manegg 4.5
the 11.1 m² Zimmer 1 opens off the living and dining space. The rule does hold
in the two German plans, where every Zimmer opens off the Diele. The corrected
version used for this run is that at least one bedroom opens off the hall, that
in flats under about 75 m² the remaining bedrooms commonly open off the living
space, and that where a separate corridor exists it is entered from the living
space rather than from the entrance.

**A bathroom opens off the hall and never off a bedroom, except one en-suite in
the largest flats. Holds, with the exception clause deleted.** Every bathroom,
shower and WC in all eight plans opens off the Entrée or the corridor. No
en-suite appears anywhere in the sample, including in the three flats at 97 to
98 m². At Zwischenbächen 4.5 the second bathroom is a second door off the
corridor. At Obsthalden the Bad and the Dusche are both off the corridor. The
corrected version is that wet rooms open only off circulation, and that a second
bathroom in a large flat is a second door off the corridor. En-suites would be
expected in market-rate rather than cooperative housing, and that was not
verified.

**The entrance opens into a hall, never straight into a bedroom. Holds, ten of
ten.** No exception was found. The Entrée can be as small as 3.7 m², and it is
always there.

## What surprised the reading

Room count does not track area. Else Züblin West publishes a 2.5-room flat at
68.9 m² and a 3.5-room flat at 68.6 m² on the same page. A configurator that
derives a room count from an area will label flats in a way no Swiss letting
agent would.

There are no en-suites in the sample at all, including at 98 m².

Bedrooms opening off the living room are normal rather than a compromise,
including in award-winning new cooperative work. This is the assumption the
reading most strongly recommends changing.

A loggia can hang off the hall. At Manegg 4.5 the second Loggia at 6.1 m² is
reached by a door from the Entrée, beside the Reduit. Outdoor space is not
necessarily a leaf of the living room.

The half room has no binding legal definition in Switzerland, and the cantons
and the federal WBS disagree with each other
(`https://www.myky.ch/de/recht/mietrecht-schweiz/zimmer-zaehlen-wohnung`).

The opposite topology exists at scale. RPLAN, the dataset of 80,788 real Asian
floor plans behind HouseGAN, treats the living room as the circulation hub, and
its generation algorithm places the living room first and hangs everything else
off it (`http://staff.ustc.edu.cn/~fuxm/projects/DeepLayout/index.html`). A
hall-centric rule set encodes a European convention, and the thesis should say
so rather than present it as neutral.

There is a Swiss ground truth large enough to settle these arguments
empirically. Swiss Dwellings (`https://zenodo.org/records/7788422`) holds 45,176
apartments, about 370,000 rooms and about 3,100 buildings, with roughly 520,000
room polygons and 715,000 openings, at a stated median area deviation of 1.2 per
cent. The derived benchmark MSD (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10121`) covers 5.3K
multi-apartment plans and 18.9K apartments. Because the openings are in the
data, every one of the four rules above could be computed from it rather than
asserted from ten plans.

## Minimum dimensions, with their sources

SIA 500 «Hindernisfreie Bauten» is the accessibility standard the app's rule A1
already refers to. The figures below are as cited by
`https://hindernisfreie-architektur.ch/fachinformation/wege-und-korridore-in-wohnbauten/`
and
`https://hindernisfreie-architektur.ch/fachinformation/tueren-fenstertueren-und-durchgaenge-in-wohnbauten/`.
The standard itself is paywalled and was not opened, so the clause numbers are
that site's.

| Item | Value | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Usable width of routes and corridors | 1.20 m | 9.3.1 |
| Straight corridor with no side exits | 1.00 m | 9.3.2 with Corrigenda C3 |
| Compensation: door clear width plus corridor width | at least 2.00 m | 9.3.2, 10.1.1 |
| Clear door or passage width | 0.80 m | 9.2.1, 10.1.1 |
| Turning space in corridors | 1.40 by 1.70 m | 9.3.3 |
| Free space beside a door leaf | 0.60 m preferred, 0.20 m absolute | 9.2.3 |
| Threshold | 0 mm preferred, 25 mm maximum | 9.2.2, 10.1.3 |

The 1.20 m corridor is the figure the app already encodes as rule A1 on the
0.6 m grid, where it reads as two cells.

On heights and areas, PBG Kanton Zürich §304 sets a clear room height of 2.40 m,
or 2.30 m in a Kernzone, with the minimum height required over at least half the
floor area in an attic storey
(`https://www.ortsplanung.ch/zh/content-pbg-304.html`). PBG §303 is reported as
setting no room smaller than 10 m², with an exception for single-family houses;
the primary text could not be opened and that figure is unverified here. BauV
Kanton Aargau §36a, at Stand September 2025, sets 2.40 m for full storeys, a
window area of at least one tenth of the floor area opening directly outside,
reducible to one fifteenth for roof windows, and ancillary rooms of 5 m² for one
and two-room units and 8 m² for larger ones
(`https://www.chkp.ch/aktuell/detailseite/news/36a-raummasse-fenstergroessen-nebenraeume`).

The WBS figures are reported from secondary summaries. The standard is at
`https://www.bwo.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/7cLO87RrIF5N/wbs_2000-d.pdf` and was not
opened, so these need checking against it before they are used as rules: living
room or Zimmer at least 12 m² in all flats; bedroom at least 8 m² for one person
and 12 m² for two; half room at least 5 m².

Both Volkswohnung plan sets state that they are barrier-free in accordance with
DIN 18040-2. That standard was not opened and its clearances are unverified
here.

For context, the Swiss average dwelling is 99 m² and the Canton Zürich average
97.2 m²; 58 per cent of units are in blocks of flats and about 70 per cent of
new construction since 2016 is flats; cooperative rents run 20 to 40 per cent
below the private sector
(`https://housingstandardisation.com/systems/switzerland`).

## What this means for the five flats

Six decisions follow from the reading and are carried into the five designs.

The entrance opens into a small entrée in every flat, and the entrée is a
circulation space rather than a room. The app requires the entrance to sit on an
outside edge, so the entrée touches the outside where the reference plans put it
against a stair core. That difference is a consequence of the app modelling one
flat with no building around it, and it is recorded rather than designed away.

Circulation is held near 10 per cent of the flat. The reference range is 5 to
15. The app's own advisory rule caps it at 25 per cent
(`src/core/rules.ts:402`, `CIRCULATION_FRACTION_MAX`), which is looser than
anything built.

The wet rooms of each flat form one block, with the kitchen against it. This is
both the prompt's hard requirement and what all eight plans do.

Every habitable room sits on the facade, and the bath, the WC and the entrée are
allowed to be internal. The app enforces the first half as must-fix rules D1 and
FAC1, so the reference plans and the rule engine agree.

At least one bedroom opens off the hall, and in the smaller flats the remaining
bedrooms open off the living room. This follows the correction to rule two
rather than the original.

No flat has an en-suite. The sample has none, and the app's rule H3 exempts
bathroom targets precisely to allow one, so leaving them out costs nothing and
matches what is built.

## Method note for the next fifteen

`WebFetch` cannot read a PDF. It returns raw binary and the summarising step
gives up. The way through is the browser pane pointed at
`https://docs.google.com/viewer?embedded=true&url=<url-encoded pdf>`, then the
viewer's own scroll and zoom. All eight labelled plans above were read that way.
The same route opens the rest of the wbg-zh datasheet series, the mehr als
wohnen Planheft at
`https://www.mehralswohnen.ch/fileadmin/downloads/Hunziker_Areal/maw_Hunziker_Areal_A_Planheft.pdf`
and the WBS standard, none of which this reading reached.
